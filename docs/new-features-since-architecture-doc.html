01Specialist staff roles

Live Backend + UI · migrations merge into existing boutiques' data

Staff used to be either a Master or a Tailor. A boutique that splits work across specialists — a separate measurer, pattern cutter, embroiderer — had nowhere to record that. Nine roles now exist, and every stage in the workflow only accepts the roles it actually calls for.

Why three stages were added

Maggam Master, Finishing Master and Pressing Staff had no stage to work on until three were added to the workflow: Maggam Work (before stitching, skippable for garments needing no embroidery), Hemming & Finishing, and Pressing (both after stitching completes). The workflow went from 12 stages to 15. A boutique run by one generalist Master is completely unaffected — every stage still accepts Master.

Stage assignment

A stage now distinguishes who should do the work from who actually did it. The owner can assign a stage to a named staff member ahead of time; the dropdown only offers staff whose role that stage permits, and the server refuses an assignment it shouldn't accept — the same rule enforced twice, once for a better experience and once because the UI can't be trusted alone.

MethodEndpointDoes
POST/orders/{id}/assign-stage/Nominate a staff member for a stage, or clear the assignment

02Design source & approval

Live Backend + UI

A saved design now records where it came from and whether it's been signed off for production — two things the order-creation wizard previously had no field for.

New inputs captured

  • Source — one of Boutique catalogue, Custom design, Previous design, Pinterest, Google images, Customer sketch, Designer sketch
  • Reference links — external inspiration URLs (Pinterest boards, image links), entered as a comma or newline separated list
  • Approval — a design can be marked approved for production, with the specific reference image that was signed off

Approving a design supersedes any design previously approved for that client, so the production checklist always has one unambiguous answer to "which design are we making."

MethodEndpointDoes
POST/customers/{id}/design-preferences/{pref_id}/approve/Sign off a design; supersedes the previous approval

In the client directory, each saved design now shows its source, an Approve for production button, and — once approved — a green "Approved for production" badge with the chosen image outlined.

03Inventory module

New Full stock foundation + screens · Not yet built per-dress material tracking, BOM, consumption

Nothing inventory-like existed before this — the closest thing, BoutiqueFabric, is a six-field price list with no quantities at all. This is a new module: an item master, an append-only stock ledger, purchasing, and the screens for all three.

The rule that makes it trustworthy

Stock quantities are never edited directly. Every change — stock in, reserve, release, issue, return, damage, scrap, or a manual count correction — goes through one service, and each writes a ledger entry recording the balance before and after, who did it, and which order and stage it relates to. A stray attempt to edit stock any other way is refused by the model itself, not just by convention.

What an item tracks

  • Item code, name, category (fabric, border, lining, embellishment, stitching material, packaging, maggam material, other), colour, unit of measure
  • Current stock, reserved stock, and available stock (derived) — kept separate because an order can claim material before it's physically issued
  • Reorder level, minimum stock, rack location, supplier, purchase and selling price

Issuing without a reservation

Boutiques often hand material to the workroom without reserving it first. Issuing now consumes whatever reservation exists and takes the remainder from free stock — only the physical balance can block an issue. (This replaced an earlier version that failed with a confusing "nothing is reserved" error on the common case.)

Purchasing

Suppliers, purchase orders with multiple lines, and partial receipt — goods can arrive in more than one delivery, and receiving books the stock-in movement and advances the order's status automatically.

Alerts

Crossing the reorder level or running out notifies the owner once per unread alert, not on every movement.

An Inventory section is now in the sidebar: a summary strip, a searchable/filterable stock table, a single movement dialog covering all eight operations, per-item history, and tabs for purchase orders and suppliers.

04What still doesn't exist

Deliberately stopped short of the harder half, because it needs a change nothing here makes yet: an order can only hold one garment. Everything below assumes a dress is its own entity with its own materials, and building it against today's one-garment-per-order would mean migrating every record once that changes.

  • Bill of materials per dress — each garment's own material list
  • Material issue tied to a specific dress, not just an order
  • Consumption reconciliation — issued vs. actually used vs. wasted
  • Customer-supplied materials as a separate ledger from boutique stock
  • Leftover material handling — return, retain, or scrap

Also unchanged from before: no email/SMS delivery for notifications (they're recorded, not sent), and the permission model on business endpoints requires authentication but has not been reviewed role-by-role for the new inventory actions.
